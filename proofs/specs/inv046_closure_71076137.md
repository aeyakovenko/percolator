# INV-046 (trade availability without unsafe mark admission): the statement in pieces (engine 71076137, program 257db166)

Status: RESTATED 2026-11-23 after the first independent read (review/inv046-read-2026-11-22/: REPORT.md, routes.tsv,
AUTHOR_CHECK.md). The read found two program defects, fixed on branch fix/inv046-trade-print-recovery-and-batch-profile
(program commit aac89b9e, landing after its gate), and seven statement errors (findings 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8). The restated clauses below describe the
program WITH that fix; the first draft, as read, is kept after them. The changed clauses need another fresh read.

The charter text (program `INVARIANTS.md`, e1295564): "A raw execution price cannot poison mark/oracle state or eliminate
every exit path. Unsafe raw prices may be ignored for discovery, safely clamped for risk/fees, or rejected only when
necessary, while at least one bounded risk-reducing route remains available." Required tests: zero, extreme, stale and
out-of-band prices during normal, drain, reset, recovery and close states.

## Second read (2026-11-23)

A second independent reader checked the restated clauses below against program 609e557b and engine 71076137. Two
findings were program defects; both are fixed in program 257db166 as the owner decided, and each has a regression test
that fails on 609e557b. The other findings correct the clauses; the corrections below supersede the restated text.

- Finding 1 (high; C5's last sentences and settled item 3 were wrong). Past a hybrid's soft-stale window, with its
  last reading still within `max_staleness_secs`, the crank re-staged the feed price T and any trade staged the
  average E; each switch bumped `oracle_epoch` and moved the target-lag penalty, which enters IM, MM and risk
  notional, between the sides, so a trader picked the target with a one-unit trade and could liquidate the side it
  chose (measured on 609e557b: 1,100,000 / 1,010,080 / 1,100,000 at oracle epochs 6, 7, 9). A crank cannot omit the
  feed to stage E (a hinted asset must carry its leg count). Fixed (the owner's rule A): past the window every writer
  stages the average until the feed publishes a newer reading. C5's last sentences now read: past the soft-stale
  window the target is the average for every writer, a crank re-reading an observed reading included; a newer reading

- Finding 4 (C6(6) and C8(ii) were wrong). Shutdown freezes a Recovery asset's `last_good_oracle_slot`, so from

  trade gates ignore a Recovery asset's own timer (`trade_resolve_matured_at_slot`); market-wide staleness still

- C3 (finding 2): the fee-supported move is `min(fee_a, fee_b)` beyond the base fee, x 10,000, over the larger of the

- C4 (finding 5): the staged target enters IM, MM and risk notional through the target-lag penalty; INV-045 bounds
  the mark's path, not the target. A print reaches the target only as the average it lands (C3's clamp), and with
  the rule of finding 1 no writer can restage a different target.
- C4 gap (finding 6, to be read): the fee basis is the raw price clamped to within cap x min(dt, W) of the average,

- C5 (finding 7, to be checked against INV-045 (b)): beyond one window behind, a failed feed read makes the crank hold

## Restated clauses (2026-11-23)

### Terms

- **Raw execution price**: the price a trade instruction carries or its LP's matcher returns: `TradeNoCpi` (tag 6),
  `TradeCpi` (tag 10, the matcher's price), `BatchTradeNoCpi` (tag 66, every leg), `BatchTradeCpi` (tag 67, every
  leg's matcher price). No other instruction takes an execution price (oracle pushes and configuration prices are
  INV-045's and INV-005's).
- **Trade-driven**: `trade_updates_mark_view(profile, group, asset, now)`: the profile is an EWMA mark, or a hybrid past
  its soft-stale window, AND the asset is Active or DrainOnly (the engine accepts a new target only there).
- **Mark/oracle state** (complete list): engine `effective_price`, `raw_oracle_target_price`, `fund_px_last` (the
  price-cap anchor), `oracle_epoch`, `slot_last`; program profile `mark_ewma_e6`, `mark_ewma_last_slot`,
  `oracle_target_price_e6`, `price_move_remainder_bps_num`, `funding_mark_e6`, `funding_mark_pending_e6/slot`,
  `effective_price_provenance`; the asset-0 mirror in the wrapper config (`mark_ewma_e6`, `mark_ewma_last_slot`,
  `oracle_target_price_e6`).
- **Risk-reducing routes**: a reducing trade (four routes, needs a counterparty); `RebalanceReduce` (the owner alone);
  liquidation (a keeper, unhealthy accounts); `ForfeitRecoveryLeg` (Recovery, forfeits junior value);
  `ForceCloseAbandonedAsset` (Recovery, after `force_close_delay_slots`); `CureAndCancelClose` (a pending close);
  `ResolveStalePermissionless` then the resolved close (terminal).

### Settled by the author after the read

- The reader's D.3 question on per-asset staleness: `RebalanceReduce` on a stale non-base asset is not blocked by the
  engine's certificate (`certify_account_after_local_settlement_with_price_override` requires each leg's B index
  current, `compute_account_health_cert_with_price_override(.., true, ..)`, not the asset accrued to the slot).
- C6 (5), a side in reset: a leg on a ResetPending side has no effective quantity (the reset settled it at the reset
  price), so there is nothing left to reduce; any public refresh settles the leg. `FinalizeResetSide` is permissionless
  (no signer check in the program handler) and succeeds once the side has no stored position, stale account, pending
  obligation or loss barrier (`finalize_side_reset_not_atomic`, returning `Stale` until then), so the wait is the
  public refresh work over that side's accounts (a liveness piece, with INV-065/INV-073).
- Finding 2 (a trade restages the average on a soft-stale hybrid): bounded, not a defect. The restaged value is the
  average, not a trader's price, and every staged change restarts the anchor (batch 15). It can at most double the
  certificate invalidations that feed-reading cranks already cause, since after one restage the engine target equals
  the average until a crank stages a feed price again.
- Settled (economics, not a write the clauses miss): whether the mark fee a trader pays to move a trade-driven average
  covers the funding the moved mark then earns a position on the receiving side while the effective price catches up
  (the reader's D.1 second question). By reading: moving the average by a fraction m costs about N m in mark fee (N the
  larger side's notional; twice that for a self-trade); the funding it earns a position of share s of that side is at
  most s N times the premium per slot, which is at most min(m, f) and lasts about m / c slots (f the funding cap per
  slot, c the price cap per slot, m <= c W by the clamp, W = max_accrual_dt_slots). So gain / fee <= s min(W / 2, f / c):
  the manipulation cannot pay when f < c (a funding cap per slot below the price cap per slot), whatever the share.
  Every admitted market has f < c: `V16Config` validation refuses `max_abs_funding_e9_per_slot > 10_000` (at most
  0.1 bps per slot) and `max_price_move_bps_per_slot == 0` (at least 1 bps per slot), so f / c <= 0.1 and the gain is
  at most a tenth of the fee (engine 71076137, the config check near line 6719). A proof piece if wanted: N.econ.
- Asides recorded for other invariants: `mark_min_fee` is a dead control (INV-087); on the CPI routes the LP's
  capability cap and the taker's signed fee are checked only against the base fee, while the dynamic mark fee can exceed
  both (INV-014, signed economic bounds).
